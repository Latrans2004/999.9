Petralysis

How concentrated is the supply of the materials an energy transition runs on?

Petralysis takes published production and trade statistics and reduces them to one number per material per year — the Herfindahl–Hirschman Index, the same measure antitrust regulators use on markets, pointed at countries instead of firms. Everything here is recomputed from source on a schedule, and every step is in the open.

What the number means

The Herfindahl–Hirschman Index is the sum of squared percentage shares. Squaring is the whole idea: it makes one supplier at 60% count for far more than six at 10% each, which is the difference a buyer actually feels when a single shipment route closes.

A monopoly scores 10,000. Ten equal suppliers score 1,000. The bands below come from the US merger guidelines and are borrowed here as a reading aid, not as a claim that countries behave like firms.

Below 1,000 Unconcentrated No supplier's absence reshapes the market on its own.
1,000 – 1,800 Moderate A handful of suppliers set the terms.
Above 1,800 Highly concentrated One or two suppliers are the market.
