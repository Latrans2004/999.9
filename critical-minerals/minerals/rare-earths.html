Traction motor magnets

Rare earths

REE

Not a cell input but a drivetrain one: neodymium–iron–boron magnets are what make a compact permanent-magnet traction motor possible. Mine output has diversified somewhat over the past decade while separation and metal-making have not, so the two indices on this page diverge more than for any other mineral here.

Mine concentration

Share of world mine production by country, from the USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries. This is where the material leaves the ground.

Export concentration

Share of world export value by reporting country, from UN Comtrade, covering Rare-earth metals (2805.30), cerium compounds (2846.10) and other rare-earth compounds (2846.90). This captures processing and refining that mine statistics do not.

What these numbers do not say

  • “Rare earths” aggregates seventeen elements with very different supply pictures; neodymium and praseodymium carry the magnet demand but cannot be separated out of these codes.
  • USGS reports rare-earth mine production as rare-earth-oxide equivalent, which is not directly comparable with the gross weight recorded in trade statistics.
  • Both indices measure countries, not companies. Two mines in different countries owned by the same parent read here as two suppliers.
