ZWE · 253090 · 2024 externally_conflicting

Three sources, no two of which agree. Zimbabwe declared 865,611 t of exports to the world in 2024, of which 688,477 t to China; China declared 1,259,180 t of imports from Zimbabwe; MMCZ put Zimbabwe's worldwide exports at 1,014,000 t. China's import declaration exceeds the figure Zimbabwe's own state marketing body publishes for exports to the entire world - an inversion no reporting lag explains. Ore smuggling is a recurring, structural problem in Zimbabwe: it has been the subject of a US House China Select Committee inquiry and of arrests over consignments as small as 720 t, and the government has gone as far as banning raw ore and concentrate exports. Large volumes are moving outside official statistics and no available source captures the real figure, so none of them is used to fill the gap.

Source checked against: Minerals Marketing Corporation of Zimbabwe (MMCZ)

Stage data and provenance

Stage weight metrics differ from the export-value index above. Country records retain reported, mirror and selected quantities, including unverified corrections and estimates.

What these numbers do not say

  • USGS withholds United States lithium production as company proprietary data, so the mine-side index is computed over the countries that do report and the missing share is stated as a coverage figure rather than imputed.
  • The trade codes cover refined lithium chemicals, not spodumene concentrate, so the export index describes the chemical conversion step rather than the mine.
  • Both indices measure countries, not companies. Two mines in different countries owned by the same parent read here as two suppliers.
