999.9/Critical minerals/Lithium

Cathode and electrolyte

Lithium

Li

The irreplaceable charge carrier in every lithium-ion cell. Mine output is dominated by Australian hard-rock spodumene and South American brine, but the refining step that turns either into battery-grade carbonate or hydroxide sits somewhere else entirely — which is why the mine-side and trade-side indices tell different stories.

Mine concentration

Share of world mine production by country, from the USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries. This is where the material leaves the ground.

Export concentration

Share of world export value by reporting country, from UN Comtrade, covering Lithium carbonate (2836.91) and lithium oxide/hydroxide (2825.20). This captures processing and refining that mine statistics do not.

What these numbers do not say

  • USGS withholds United States lithium production as company proprietary data, so the mine-side index is computed over the countries that do report and the missing share is stated as a coverage figure rather than imputed.
  • The trade codes cover refined lithium chemicals, not spodumene concentrate, so the export index describes the chemical conversion step rather than the mine.
  • Both indices measure countries, not companies. Two mines in different countries owned by the same parent read here as two suppliers.
